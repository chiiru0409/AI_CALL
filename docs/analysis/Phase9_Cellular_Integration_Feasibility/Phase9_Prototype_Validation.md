   - **ADB Connection:** Active and verified (`10BE7U31G8000R6 device`).
   - **ADB Reverse Port Forwarding:** Reverse tunnel `tcp:8000 -> tcp:8000` (`UsbFfs tcp:8000 tcp:8000`) was re-established and verified.
   - **Ollama Engine:** Active on loopback (`http://127.0.0.1:11434`), model `qwen2.5:3b` (1.9 GB, SHA256 `357c53fb...`) verified resident and responsive.
3. **Network Security & Binding Audit:**
   - `server.py` currently binds to `0.0.0.0:8000` with **zero authentication**. Any device on the local Wi-Fi subnet (`192.168.0.x`) can access the endpoint.
   - Furthermore, plain HTTP access over a LAN IP (`http://192.168.0.186:8000`) causes Chromium on Android to **block** `navigator.mediaDevices.getUserMedia` because it violates W3C Secure Context rules.

   - **Security Recommendation:** To prevent unauthorized LAN access, `server.py` should be updated to bind strictly to loopback (`127.0.0.1:8000`).

## 5. Discrepancies Between Benchmark and Real-Device Reality

1. **Acoustic Reverberation vs. Digital WAVs:** SAPI Speech Recognizer 8.0 exhibits high error rates on conversational acoustic microphone speech compared to synthetic audio. In testing, clean synthetic audio of common English phrases had transcription errors ("Chiru" -> "gyro", "speak" -> "begin"). Live acoustic speech in a real room will require a more robust offline English recognizer (e.g. `faster-whisper base.en`).
2. **Audio Playback Latency:** While synthesis latency on the PC is ~130ms, the mobile browser must receive the base64-encoded WAV over WebSocket, decode the Blob, instantiate an `HTMLAudioElement`, and wait for the Android audio hal to buffer and route the stream to the physical speaker. Real-world end-to-end turnaround is approximately **2.0 – 2.5 seconds**, rather than the raw 1.67s server turnaround.
3. **No Cellular Voice Connection:** Successfully streaming microphone audio to a PC web server and playing synthesized audio back through the phone loudspeaker **does not alter the cellular modem uplink**. It confirms only that the phone can act as a local IP audio peripheral over USB/Wi-Fi.
